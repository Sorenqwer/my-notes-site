Glossary

This glossary defines recurring terms used across the criminology notes. The definitions are intentionally compact. They are meant to support reading, not replace full theory pages.

Core concepts

Crime

Behaviour prohibited by criminal law and subject to formal sanction.

Deviance

Behaviour that violates social norms. Deviance is not always criminal, and crime is not always treated as equally deviant.

Harm

Damage or injury to persons, groups, communities, institutions, or social relations. Some harmful acts are not criminalized.

Criminalization

The process through which behaviour becomes defined and treated as criminal.

Social control

Formal or informal processes that regulate behaviour. Formal social control includes police, courts, and law. Informal social control includes family, peers, communities, and norms.

Victimization

The experience of being harmed by crime or deviant behaviour.

Offending

Engaging in behaviour defined as criminal or rule-breaking.

Desistance

The process through which a person stops offending over time.

Measurement terms

True crime

The actual amount of crime that occurs, whether or not it is reported or recorded.

Recorded crime

Crime that appears in official records.

Hidden crime

Crime that occurs but does not appear in official statistics.

Reporting

The process by which a victim, witness, or institution communicates an event to authorities.

Detection

The process by which authorities discover an offence.

Recording

The institutional classification and entry of an event into official data.

Prevalence

The proportion of a population experiencing or engaging in an event during a period.

Incidence

The number of events occurring during a period.

Rate

A count normalized by population, exposure, time, or opportunity.

Clearance

A case outcome indicating that an offence has been resolved or attributed according to official criteria. Clearance does not always mean conviction.

Mechanism terms

Deterrence

The idea that expected punishment can reduce offending by increasing expected costs.

Certainty

The perceived or actual probability of punishment.

Severity

The harshness of punishment if imposed.

Celerity

The speed with which punishment follows the offence.

Incapacitation

Crime reduction by physically preventing a person from offending, often through custody or restriction.

Rehabilitation

Intervention aimed at changing skills, constraints, behaviour, or circumstances associated with offending.

Routine activity

The idea that crime occurs when a motivated offender, suitable target, and lack of capable guardianship converge.

Guardianship

Presence of people, institutions, technologies, or conditions that reduce opportunity for crime.

Opportunity

A situational condition that makes offending easier, more attractive, or less risky.

Self-control

Capacity to regulate impulses, delay gratification, and consider longer-term consequences.

Present bias

Overweighting immediate rewards or costs relative to future consequences.

Peer effect

Influence of peers on behaviour through learning, pressure, imitation, approval, or opportunity.

Social learning

Learning behaviour, motives, techniques, or justifications through interaction with others.

Labeling

The process by which official or social reaction changes identity, status, opportunity, or future behaviour.

Stigma

Negative social marking that can reduce opportunity, trust, and social standing.

Legitimacy

The perceived rightfulness of an authority, law, or institution.

Procedural justice

Fairness of decision-making processes, including voice, neutrality, respect, and trustworthy motives.

Compliance

Behaviour that follows rules, laws, or institutional expectations.

Institution terms

Police discretion

The room police have to decide whether and how to intervene, record, warn, arrest, or escalate.

Prosecution

The legal process of bringing charges against a defendant.

Plea bargaining

Negotiation in which a defendant may plead guilty in exchange for concessions, reduced charges, or sentencing expectations.

Conviction

A formal legal finding of guilt.

Sentencing

The formal assignment of punishment or legal consequence after conviction.

Probation

Community-based supervision, usually imposed instead of or after custody.

Parole

Conditional release from custody under supervision.

Reentry

The process of returning to community life after incarceration.

Recidivism

Reoffending after prior contact with the criminal justice system.

Model terms

Actor

A person, institution, group, or organization that makes choices in a model.

Choice

An action available to an actor.

Information

What an actor knows or observes when choosing.

Payoff

What an actor gains, loses, values, or avoids.

Strategy

A complete plan of action, especially in strategic settings.

Equilibrium

A stable pattern of behaviour in which actors have no relevant incentive to deviate, given the model.

Inspection game

A model in which one actor may violate a rule and another actor may inspect or monitor.

Reporting game

A model in which a victim or witness decides whether to report, often depending on trust, cost, and expected response.

Network model

A model where relationships among actors affect behaviour, diffusion, opportunity, or enforcement.

Displacement

A change in crime location, time, target, method, or type after an intervention.

Diffusion of benefits

A reduction in crime beyond the directly targeted area or group.

Common contrasts

Term 1 Term 2 Difference
Crime Deviance Legal violation versus norm violation
True crime Recorded crime Actual events versus official records
Incidence Prevalence Number of events versus share affected
Deterrence Incapacitation Discouraging behaviour versus physically preventing it
Rehabilitation Punishment Changing behaviour versus imposing sanction
Legitimacy Fear Voluntary acceptance versus coercive compliance
Description Explanation Pattern identification versus mechanism
