Notes

This section collects the main technical notes of the site. The focus is on strategic reasoning, equilibrium concepts, model structure, and the mathematical tools needed to read game-theoretic arguments carefully.

The notes are organised around three layers:

  1. Game types: what kind of strategic environment we are studying.
  2. Equilibrium concepts: what it means for behaviour to be stable or rational.
  3. Metrics and foundations: how we measure, compare, and formalise objects in the model.

Map of the notes section

This map is meant as a guide rather than a strict reading order. In practice, the same model often involves several layers at once. For example, a signaling game is both a game type, an information problem, and an equilibrium problem.

How to use these notes

Game theory is easiest to learn when every page is read with four questions in mind:

  1. Who are the players?
  2. What can they do?
  3. What do they know?
  4. Why is the proposed behaviour stable?

Most mistakes in game-theoretic reasoning come from skipping one of these questions. A model may look formal, but if the players, strategies, information, or payoffs are unclear, the equilibrium result will also be unclear.
