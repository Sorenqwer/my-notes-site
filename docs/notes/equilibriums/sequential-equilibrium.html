Sequential Equilibrium

Sequential equilibrium is a refinement for dynamic games with incomplete information. It strengthens perfect Bayesian equilibrium by requiring strategies to be sequentially rational and beliefs to be consistent as limits of fully mixed strategies.

The central idea is:

players act optimally at every information set, and beliefs should be justified by nearby completely mixed behaviour.

Why sequential equilibrium matters

Perfect Bayesian equilibrium requires sequential rationality and Bayes’ rule where possible. But off-path beliefs may still be too flexible.

Sequential equilibrium adds stronger discipline to beliefs.

This makes sequential equilibrium useful when off-path beliefs matter.

Assessment

A sequential equilibrium is defined as an assessment:

\[ (\sigma,\mu), \]

where:

Object Meaning
\(\sigma\) strategy profile
\(\mu\) system of beliefs

The assessment must satisfy:

  1. sequential rationality;
  2. consistency.

Sequential rationality

Sequential rationality means every player chooses optimally at every information set, given beliefs and continuation strategies.

For each information set, the prescribed strategy must maximize expected continuation payoff.

This is similar to PBE.

Consistency

Consistency is the stronger belief requirement.

Beliefs should be obtainable as limits of beliefs generated by completely mixed strategy profiles.

A completely mixed strategy assigns positive probability to every action. This ensures every information set is reached with positive probability, so Bayes’ rule can be applied everywhere.

Then we take a limit as trembles go to zero.

This prevents arbitrary off-path beliefs that cannot be justified by small mistakes.

Sequential equilibrium versus PBE

Concept Sequential rationality Belief requirement
PBE yes Bayes’ rule on path; off-path beliefs often flexible
Sequential equilibrium yes beliefs must be consistent limits of fully mixed strategies

Every sequential equilibrium is generally a PBE, but not every PBE is sequential.

Why completely mixed strategies?

If every action has positive probability, then every information set can be reached.

That means beliefs can always be computed by Bayes’ rule.

The idea is that even if an action is not supposed to occur in equilibrium, it could occur as a small mistake or tremble.

Beliefs after unexpected actions should be consistent with the relative likelihood of such mistakes.

Example intuition

Suppose a receiver observes an unexpected signal.

PBE may allow many possible beliefs after that signal.

Sequential equilibrium asks:

If the unexpected signal occurred because of very small mistakes, which type would be more likely to make that mistake?

This gives off-path beliefs more structure.

Relation to trembling-hand perfection

Both sequential equilibrium and trembling-hand perfection use the idea of small mistakes, but in different ways.

Concept Main object
Trembling-hand perfect equilibrium robustness of strategies to small mistakes
Sequential equilibrium consistency of beliefs and sequential rationality
Proper equilibrium relative likelihood of more costly mistakes

Sequential equilibrium is especially designed for extensive-form games with imperfect information.

Beliefs at information sets

In an extensive-form game, an information set may contain several decision nodes.

A belief assigns probabilities to these nodes.

For example, if information set \(I\) contains nodes \(x\) and \(y\), beliefs may be:

\[ \mu(x\mid I)=0.6, \]

and

\[ \mu(y\mid I)=0.4. \]

The player’s optimal action depends on expected payoff under these beliefs.

Sequential equilibrium in signaling

In a signaling game, sequential equilibrium can restrict off-path beliefs after unexpected signals.

For example, in a pooling equilibrium, both types send the same signal. If the receiver observes an unexpected signal, PBE may allow arbitrary beliefs. Sequential equilibrium asks whether those beliefs can be justified by small probabilities of each type deviating.

Why it is stronger

Sequential equilibrium is stronger because it connects beliefs to possible trembles.

It rules out some equilibria that rely on unreasonable beliefs after unexpected events.

However, it can also be more technically demanding than PBE.

Common mistakes

1. Treating sequential equilibrium as the same as PBE

Sequential equilibrium has a stronger consistency requirement.

2. Ignoring beliefs

Sequential equilibrium is an assessment: strategies plus beliefs.

3. Thinking consistency means only Bayes’ rule on path

Consistency also disciplines off-path beliefs through limits.

4. Forgetting sequential rationality

Beliefs alone are not enough. Actions must be optimal given beliefs.

5. Confusing trembles with actual behaviour

Trembles are a refinement device, not necessarily a literal behavioural claim.

Checklist

When checking sequential equilibrium, ask:

  1. What is the strategy profile?
  2. What beliefs are assigned at each information set?
  3. Are strategies sequentially rational given beliefs?
  4. Can beliefs be generated by fully mixed strategies?
  5. Do those fully mixed strategies converge to the proposed strategy?
  6. Do the induced beliefs converge to the proposed beliefs?
  7. Are off-path beliefs justified by this limiting process?
  8. Is the assessment also a PBE?
  9. What equilibria are ruled out by consistency?
  10. Is the refinement substantively important in this model?
