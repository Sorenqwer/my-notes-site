Babbling Equilibrium

A babbling equilibrium is a cheap-talk equilibrium in which messages carry no information. The sender may still send messages, but the receiver ignores them.

The central idea is:

communication occurs, but it does not change beliefs or actions.

Babbling is important because it shows that costless communication is not automatically informative.

Basic structure

In a cheap-talk game:

  1. the sender observes private information;
  2. the sender sends a message;
  3. the receiver observes the message;
  4. the receiver chooses an action.

In a babbling equilibrium, the receiver’s action does not depend on the message.

The message is strategically irrelevant.

Definition

A babbling equilibrium is an equilibrium where:

  1. the receiver’s belief after each on-path message is the same as the prior;
  2. the receiver chooses the same action regardless of message;
  3. the sender has no incentive to choose a different message because messages do not affect the receiver’s action.

If the receiver ignores all messages, then the sender is indifferent among messages.

Why babbling is an equilibrium

Babbling is self-confirming.

Because the receiver does not respond to messages, the sender cannot benefit from carefully choosing a message. Since the sender does not choose messages informatively, the receiver is right to ignore them.

Prior-based action

If the receiver ignores the message, the receiver chooses the action that maximizes expected payoff under the prior belief.

If the state is \(\theta\), and the receiver chooses action \(a\), then the receiver solves:

\[ a^* \in \arg\max_{a\in A} \sum_{\theta} u_R(a,\theta)\Pr(\theta). \]

The message does not enter the receiver’s decision.

Beliefs

In a babbling equilibrium, on-path messages do not change beliefs.

For message \(m\):

\[ \mu(\theta\mid m)=\Pr(\theta). \]

The posterior equals the prior.

This is what it means for the message to carry no information.

Example intuition

Suppose an expert knows whether a project is good or bad. The decision-maker must decide whether to approve it.

If the expert always wants approval, regardless of the true project quality, then the expert may always say:

The project is good.

The decision-maker anticipates this and ignores the message.

The message becomes babble.

Babbling and pooling

Babbling is related to pooling, but they are not identical.

In a pooling equilibrium, multiple types send the same message.

In a babbling equilibrium, the receiver’s action does not depend on the message.

Concept Meaning
Pooling different types choose the same message or signal
Babbling messages do not affect beliefs or actions

A pooling equilibrium can be informative if the pooled group still changes beliefs relative to some other message. Babbling is fully uninformative.

Babbling and cheap talk

Babbling is especially common in cheap-talk games because messages are costless and non-binding.

If sender and receiver preferences are too misaligned, informative communication may fail. The receiver ignores messages, and the sender cannot credibly transmit information.

Always exists intuition

In many cheap-talk games, a babbling equilibrium always exists.

The logic is:

  1. receiver ignores messages;
  2. sender messages do not affect payoffs;
  3. sender is willing to send any message;
  4. receiver’s belief that messages are uninformative is confirmed.

This does not mean babbling is the only equilibrium. Informative equilibria may also exist when preferences are sufficiently aligned.

Babbling versus informative equilibrium

Feature Babbling Informative communication
Message changes belief? no yes
Message changes action? no yes
Sender strategy depends on type? not meaningfully yes
Receiver learns from message? no partly or fully
Requires aligned interests? no usually some alignment

Babbling is the baseline. Informative communication requires additional incentive structure.

Off-path messages

Off-path messages are messages that are not expected in equilibrium.

In babbling equilibria, off-path beliefs can matter if a receiver might react strongly to an unexpected message.

A proposed babbling equilibrium should specify:

  • what beliefs the receiver holds after each message;
  • what action the receiver takes after each message;
  • why the sender does not want to deviate.

Babbling and real communication

Babbling does not mean people are literally silent or meaningless. It means the message does not change strategic beliefs or actions.

Real examples may include:

  • empty campaign promises;
  • routine corporate statements;
  • excuses known to be self-serving;
  • warnings from biased sources;
  • cheap apologies without behavioural consequence;
  • unverifiable claims from interested parties.

The words may have social meaning, but in the model they transmit no payoff-relevant information.

Common mistakes

1. Thinking babbling means no message is sent

Messages may be sent. They are just ignored.

2. Thinking babbling means irrationality

Babbling can be fully rational in equilibrium.

3. Confusing babbling with lying

Babbling is about informativeness, not necessarily truth or falsehood.

4. Ignoring receiver action

Babbling requires the receiver’s action not to depend on the message.

5. Assuming babbling is the only possible cheap-talk equilibrium

Informative cheap-talk equilibria may exist under sufficient preference alignment.

Checklist

When checking a babbling equilibrium, ask:

  1. What information does the sender observe?
  2. What messages are available?
  3. Does the receiver’s belief change after messages?
  4. Does the receiver’s action change after messages?
  5. Is the receiver’s action optimal under the prior?
  6. Is the sender indifferent among messages?
  7. Could any message profitably change the receiver’s action?
  8. Are off-path beliefs specified?
  9. Are there possible informative equilibria too?
  10. What does babbling mean substantively in this case?
