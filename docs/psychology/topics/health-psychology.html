Health psychology

Common UG elective.

Health psychology studies how psychological mechanisms shape: - health behaviors (sleep, exercise, diet, substance use), - stress and coping, - adherence to treatment, - risk perception and health communication.

A useful framing: > Many “health problems” are behavior + environment problems, mediated by attention, habit, emotion, and social norms.

1) A simple model of health behavior

Health behavior is shaped by:

  1. Capabilities (skills, executive control, knowledge)
  2. Opportunities (access, time, cost, environment cues)
  3. Motivation (goals, identity, reward/relief)
  4. Feedback (immediate vs delayed consequences)
  5. Social context (norms, support, stigma)

If you ignore opportunities and feedback timing, you’ll over-blame “willpower.”

2) Stress and coping

Stress changes: - attention (tunnel vision), - time horizon (short-run relief dominates), - emotion regulation (irritability, avoidance), - habit expression (defaults take over).

Coping strategies often split into: - problem-focused (change the situation), - emotion-focused (change how it feels), - avoidant (escape, short-run relief).

Prediction patterns - avoidant coping reduces distress short-run but maintains long-run problems (avoidance loops). - stable routines reduce stress reactivity over time.

3) Health beliefs and risk perception

People do not perceive risk objectively. Risk perception is shaped by: - availability (vivid cases dominate), - framing (relative vs absolute risk), - trust in source, - emotion (fear/anger), - numeracy and comprehension (without blaming individuals).

Prediction patterns - narratives move behavior more than statistics when trust is low. - trust in institutions predicts compliance with guidance.

Intervention lever: improve clarity + credibility rather than only increasing information volume.

4) Adherence: why people don’t follow treatment plans

Non-adherence can arise from: - forgetting (attention/retrieval failure), - low expectancy (“this won’t help”), - side effects and immediate costs, - stigma and identity threat, - lack of access (structural constraint), - mistrust in providers/institutions.

Prediction patterns - reminders at the right time increase adherence even without changing beliefs. - respectful treatment and voice increase adherence through legitimacy.

5) Habit and cue control (the real workhorse)

Health behaviors are strongly cue-driven: - time-of-day routines, - location cues (kitchen, bed, phone), - emotional cues (stress → snack, anxiety → avoidance).

Prediction patterns - environment redesign outperforms motivation-only interventions. - small frictions (e.g., making unhealthy choices harder) have large effects.

6) Behavior change tools (evidence-aligned, non-clinical)

Linking to your toolkit page, common high-leverage tools include:

  • Implementation intentions (“If it’s 10pm, I start wind-down.”)
  • Defaults and automation (pre-scheduling, subscriptions, medication organizers)
  • Commitment devices (public plans, deposits, accountability)
  • Self-monitoring (tracking as attention support, not moral judgment)
  • Social support (norms and belonging)
  • Choice architecture (reduce choice overload; reduce friction for desired action)

Prediction pattern: tools work best when they act at the cue stage, not at the “willpower stage.”

7) Placebo, expectations, and meaning (high-level)

Expectations can change: - perceived symptoms, - pain and discomfort, - engagement and adherence.

This is not “fake”; it reflects how brain/body interpret signals under uncertainty.

Prediction pattern: credible rituals and trusted providers increase expectancy effects.

8) Health inequalities (environment + stress + access)

Health outcomes correlate with: - chronic stress exposure, - neighborhood environment, - access to care, - time scarcity, - stigma and institutional trust.

Prediction pattern: interventions that ignore structural constraints may widen inequality (only the well-resourced can comply).

A reusable health-psych analysis template

When analyzing a health behavior, specify:

  1. Behavior (what exactly?)
  2. Cue (when/where/with whom/which emotion)
  3. Reinforcement (reward or relief; immediate vs delayed)
  4. Constraints (time, money, access, stigma)
  5. Beliefs (expectancy, trust, risk perception)
  6. Social context (norms, support)
  7. Intervention (which lever acts earliest in the loop?)
