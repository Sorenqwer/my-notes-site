Mental health

This page is a foundational, non-clinical map of mental health topics that matter for understanding behavior. It is not a diagnostic guide. The goal is to organize mechanisms and predictions in a way that is useful for study and for linking to other parts of the site (decision-making, learning, emotion, social cognition).

A helpful framing: > Mental health patterns often reflect interactions between vulnerabilities, environments, and coping strategies.

Core dimensions (a practical map)

Many symptoms can be grouped by a few dimensions:

  • Negative affect: persistent sadness, anxiety, guilt, irritability
  • Threat sensitivity: hypervigilance, avoidance, panic responses
  • Reward processing: reduced pleasure, low motivation, anhedonia
  • Cognitive control: attention, impulsivity, rumination, executive function
  • Sleep and arousal: insomnia, fatigue, agitation
  • Social functioning: isolation, mistrust, rejection sensitivity

Different “disorders” often share dimensions. That’s part of why comorbidity is common.

Stress and vulnerability: a general mechanism

A broad mechanism often used is:

  • Vulnerability (traits, history, genetics, learned patterns)
  • Stressors (acute shocks, chronic strain, social conflict)
  • Protective factors (support, coping skills, stable routines)

Prediction pattern: the same stressor affects people differently depending on vulnerability and protection.

Anxiety: threat and uncertainty

Anxiety is often characterized by: - heightened threat perception, - intolerance of uncertainty, - avoidance behavior, - safety-seeking behaviors that reduce anxiety short-run but maintain it long-run.

Learning loop (common): - uncertainty → anxiety → avoidance → temporary relief → stronger avoidance habit

Prediction pattern: reducing avoidance and increasing safe exposure often weakens the loop over time (mechanism: learning + prediction error).

Depression: reward, energy, and self-evaluation

Depressive patterns often involve: - reduced motivation and energy, - diminished reward sensitivity (things feel less rewarding), - negative self-evaluation, - rumination (repetitive negative thinking).

Behavioral loop (common): - low mood → withdrawal → fewer rewarding experiences → lower mood

Prediction pattern: small increases in structured rewarding activity can have outsized effects because they interrupt the loop (mechanism: reinforcement and habit).

Rumination: repetitive thought as a trap

Rumination feels like problem-solving but often functions as: - attention capture, - negative memory retrieval, - threat rehearsal, - reduced action initiation.

Prediction pattern: interventions that shift attention (structured activity, reappraisal skills, sleep stabilization) reduce rumination frequency.

Trauma-related patterns: memory, threat, and triggers

After extreme stress, some people develop: - intrusive memories, - hypervigilance, - avoidance of reminders, - emotional numbing, - sleep disruption.

Mechanism lens: emotion-tagged memory + threat system sensitization + avoidance learning.

Prediction pattern: unpredictable triggers and uncertain environments maintain hypervigilance.

ADHD-like patterns (non-diagnostic framing)

Common behavioral dimensions include: - inattention under low stimulation, - difficulty sustaining effort, - impulsivity (short-horizon choice), - strong sensitivity to immediate rewards.

Mechanism lens: attention regulation + reinforcement sensitivity + executive control load.

Prediction pattern: structure, feedback frequency, and reduced friction can help performance even without changing “traits.”

Substance use and compulsive behavior (mechanism lens)

A compact mechanism view: - strong immediate reward/relief, - habit formation, - cue-triggered craving, - withdrawal/negative reinforcement, - social context and norms.

Prediction pattern: changing cue environments and social networks often matters as much as willpower.

Stigma and social context (often overlooked)

Mental health is shaped by: - perceived acceptance vs rejection, - shame and concealment, - access to support and care, - institutional treatment (school, workplace, legal system).

Prediction pattern: reducing stigma and increasing safe disclosure channels increases help-seeking and reduces isolation loops.

A reusable explanation template

When discussing a mental health-related behavior, specify:

  1. Dimension(s) involved (threat, reward, control, sleep, social)
  2. Loop (avoidance loop? withdrawal loop? rumination loop?)
  3. Context (stressors, stability, social support)
  4. Prediction (what change would interrupt the loop?)
  5. Boundaries (what you are not claiming; avoid diagnosis)

Connections

  • Emotion & motivation: affective systems and action tendencies are central.
  • Learning: avoidance and reinforcement loops maintain patterns.
  • Cognition: attention and memory biases shape symptoms.
  • Norms & groups: stigma and identity can worsen or buffer outcomes.
  • Criminology (later link): trauma exposure, substance use, and stress can affect compliance, escalation, and institutional interactions.
